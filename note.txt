Regarding your research topic, I would like to suggest a study on physical-contact HRI,
represented by massage robots. The main idea is to investigate how a robot can understand
the physical state of a contacted body part through touch and adapt its contact behavior accordingly.

As the first step, please try measuring your own finger and hand motions using the Manus Grove device
available in our laboratory. This will help you become familiar with motion measurement and understand
how human hand movements can be recorded and analyzed.

After that, we can prepare two simple body-part models: one representing a hard region and the other representing a soft region.

Then, we will measure how a human therapist touches each model, including contact force, pressing motion, pressing speed, contact position, and motion timing.

By analyzing these data, we will try to clarify how human touch behavior changes depending on the stiffness of the contacted body part.

The goal is to develop a basic HRI system in which the robot first recognizes whether the contacted region is hard or soft from contact information, and then reproduces an appropriate touch motion for each condition.

In other words, the robot should not only “touch” the human body, but also interact through touch by sensing the physical condition of the body and changing its behavior.

As a rough plan for the two-month internship, we can proceed from basic motion measurement with Manus Grove, then move on to the preparation of hard and soft body-part models, a small measurement experiment, data analysis, and discussion of robot behavior design.

If time allows, we may also implement a simple robot motion based on the measured data.

Through this project, you can experience the full research flow: experimental setup, human motion measurement, data analysis, and robot behavior design.
